### Fixes for the November 22 evaluation

- Movie decoding applies the selected track's presentation end before rate conversion or encoding. Single-threaded movie decoding keeps a leading skip on the correct frame. A lossless cut at 62,400 samples excludes all 3,136 samples of the second tone, and a combined leading and trailing cut matches the authored slice. The native duration of a fragmented upload grows during demuxing. The check follows that duration through the last fragment.
- A second investigation found that the native MOV demuxer can restart a later edit on the wrong sample. ## Performance follow-ups

The [audio benchmark](audio-processing-benchmark.md) retains numerical comparisons and reproduction commands. Both scheduling and startup follow-ups are implemented; buffer trimming remains an unselected tradeoff:

- **PCM scheduling implemented:** finite workers have individual ordered queues within the existing logical-processor/four-worker cap. Resampling submits bounded 262144-sample jobs and yields its place between them; short operations progress even on one core. A shared iterator preserves one input-copy budget and limits prepared work. Other operations prepare input only in their queued turn. Streams remain independently owned; empty results bypass workers. Full offline/coverage, native, Node/browser and affected mutation checks pass. The measured short-result median is 0.7 ms versus 59.4 ms for a serialized control on this machine; this is not a latency guarantee.
- **Native reuse implemented:** one cleaned worker may remain idle for five seconds. Each invocation has its own port/resources/settings/signal; overlapping calls start separate workers. Failed/cancelled calls and excess/expired idle workers exit gracefully. The [measured comparison](audio-processing-benchmark.md#queue-and-native-startup-measurements-2026-11-17) shows a substantial startup reduction without changing the PCM queue.
- Prepared-file buffers grow geometrically. Native probes returned 9,678 / 72,078 / 1,440,078 bytes backed by 65,536 / 131,072 / 2,097,152 bytes. `maxBytes` caps capacity; the byte view excludes spare capacity from uploads. Capacity remains while bytes are reachable. An exact-size copy adds work and temporarily retains both allocations, increasing peak memory.

Buffer trimming is an unselected tradeoff, not scheduled work: a retained-capacity saving does not establish a net benefit after extra copying and peak memory. Measure sustained workloads before changing the selected scheduling policy or adding an obligatory output copy. Heavy work stays off the calling thread regardless of throughput. The four-worker cap is a budget, not a universal optimum.
